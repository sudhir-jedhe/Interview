They lose because small mistakes quietly weaken an otherwise strong profile.

An interview is not only about what you know. It is also about how clearly you think, how well you communicate, and how professionally you show up from start to finish.

Some of the most common mistakes are surprisingly simple:

↳ Weak role preparation and poor company research
↳ Generic answers that could fit any job
↳ Repeating resume points without adding context or impact
↳ Unstructured responses with no clear story
↳ Claims without evidence, examples, or results
↳ Talking too much and missing the main point
↳ Not listening carefully or failing to ask clarifying questions
↳ Blaming others or sounding negative about past teams
↳ Overconfidence or pretending to know everything
↳ Hiding knowledge gaps instead of being honest
↳ Weak closing questions and no thoughtful follow-up

What makes these mistakes costly is that they shape perception.

A strong interviewer is not only checking skill.

They are also asking:

-Can this person communicate clearly?
-Can they think under pressure?
-Can they stay honest, calm, and structured?

Would I trust them with real responsibility?

That is why preparation matters beyond technical knowledge.

Good candidates prepare stories, research the company, listen fully, answer with structure, back up claims with proof, and end the conversation with curiosity and professionalism.
Interviews are rarely won by perfect answers.

They are won by clarity, relevance, self-awareness, and trust.

Sometimes removing a few avoidable mistakes creates a bigger improvement than learning ten new answers.
